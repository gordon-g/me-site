1. Medical AI skill libraries reach "thousand-item" scale — OpenClaw-Medical-Skills and scientific-agent-skills land the same day

With 869 medical agent skills this is the largest open medical skill library to date, spanning biomedical and clinical workflows; a second library covering verified scientific skills appeared the same day. The competition has moved from "which model" to "who ships the skill pack plus MCP servers".

2. On-device clinical de-identification — OpenMed makes HIPAA compliance a local capability

Repo: https://github.com/maziyarpanahi/openmed

Clinical NER plus HIPAA PII de-identification, running 100% on-device (Apple MLX + Python), bundling 2,200+ medical models across 21 languages, with data never leaving the intranet.

3. Food and nutrition AI moves from "logging" to "auditing" — FoodTruth uses a vision model to break packaging claims

Repo: https://github.com/dasouvik122005/FoodTruth

An open-weight vision model reads both the front-of-pack claims and the back-of-pack ingredient and nutrition panels, then flags three classes of problem: hidden sugar (a "no added sugar" product containing maltodextrin with a GI of 110), "whey-protein dusting" claims, and similar wording tricks.

4. GitHub turns Copilot from "picking a model" into "orchestrating models" — HydraFusion Max

GitHub's 10-14 update to Copilot's HydraFusion orchestration added a Max tier, refreshed the pool (Claude Opus 5.5 / GPT-6 Astra / GPT-6.1 Sol / GPT-6 Luna), and allocates reasoning effort by stage — medium for drafting, high for review — with cascading fallbacks.

5. WiFi becomes a health sensor — RuView monitors vitals without a camera

Repo: https://github.com/ruvnet/RuView

Turns ordinary commercial WiFi signals into real-time spatial intelligence: breathing, heart rate and presence detection with zero video capture. It gained 197 stars on the day and represents a rare "privacy at no cost" route for health monitoring in bedrooms and bathrooms.

Trends

  1. The skill becomes the unit of distribution. In both coding and medicine, competition has shifted from models to "skill pack + MCP server"; the medical side already has an 869-item library.
  2. Local-first is the default in health. OpenMed, FoodYou and RuView all insist data stays on the device — regulatory pressure is reshaping technology choices.
  3. Diet AI is upgrading from a logging tool to a judgement tool. Deterministic nutrition rules (GI, DIAAS, regulatory citations) are being hard-coded into audit engines instead of left to the model to improvise.
  4. Open weights keep accelerating. Meta Muse Spark Open (816B) and StepFun Step 5 Preview (600B) both opened weights the same day, both aimed explicitly at long-horizon agent tasks.
  5. Open-source supply is outpacing consumption. Agent orchestration, skill libraries and context compression dominate Trending; selection and governance — security, audit, context cost — are becoming the new bottleneck.
