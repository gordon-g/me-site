1. There needs to be a cognitive layer beside the model: Empirica uses 13 vectors and a Sentinel gate to turn an agent's overconfidence into something interceptable

URL: https://github.com/EmpiricaAI/empirica ｜ ★257 ｜ MIT ｜ a reliability-measurement framework that sits beside the model: 13 epistemic vectors, evidence-tiered four-layer memory, a Sentinel gate before acting, and confidence calibrated against deterministic checks

What happened

  • 25 commits today and a v1.17.18 release (2026-11-25 15:17). The scale figures in the README are 13 epistemic vectors, 240+ CLI commands, 26 skills shipped with the plugin, and 600+ real working sessions as the empirical basis — note that the README states plainly that those 600+ sessions have no published dataset or sampling method, so they cannot be treated as a public benchmark.
  • The architecture is a transactional loop: each task is split into a transaction that runs PREFLIGHT (what do I know now) → CHECK (am I ready to act) → POSTFLIGHT (what did I learn). The NOETIC phase investigates, searches and reads, recording findings, unknowns and dead-ends; the PRAXIC phase implements; the Sentinel sits between the two.
  • The Sentinel gate uses domain-aware thresholds scaled by criticality, not one fixed threshold. The README's line is that "cybersec/high is stricter than default/low", and the thresholds tighten further when the model's own calibration is poor. If CHECK does not pass, the agent should keep investigating rather than start changing code.
  • Confidence calibration uses a three-vector model: self-assessed, observed (from deterministic checks) and an AI-reasoned grounded state with rationale. The test it applies is whether a previously expressed confidence is supported by results from tests, lint and static analysis — not how confident the model's tone sounds.
  • The memory layer models findings, dead-ends, assumptions, mistakes, decisions, unknowns and falsifiers separately, each carrying provenance and confidence, with stale or wrong content retractable and supersedable, and retrieval done by task relevance via semantic matching. The epistemic-gardening skill in particular resolves, retracts and prunes the artifact graph before publishing.
  • Forecasts are not plain plan items but falsifiable records carrying direction, confidence, supporting evidence, and what would refute them, scored when they land or fail. Evidence strength is graded measured / reviewed / reported / inferred, and the README's rule is that "weaker evidence cannot carry a higher confidence".

Why it matters

  • This one answers head-on the question that has run through my work for half a year: what actually enforces my "zero AI hallucination" rule. I have written "mandatory evidence grading + references + zero AI hallucination" on every deliverable, but it has always been a self-imposed requirement rather than an executable mechanism. Empirica turns it into three fields you can actually ship: an evidence tier per claim, explicit records of what is still unknown, and a gate before acting. Translated to my own projects, that means three concrete things: every number in an intervention report carries an evidence grade, the parts of a plan I have not verified are written down rather than left blank, and before recommending a supplement I check that I genuinely hold its contraindication and interaction data — which is what a Sentinel gate looks like in nutrition intervention.
  • "How confident the model's tone sounds is not evidence" lands precisely on where my public-account content most often goes wrong. The easiest thing I lose in retelling research is the qualifier: small sample, single population, observational design — all of it crystal clear in the original, all of it gone after paraphrase. The three-vector calibration model gives an operable criterion: when I write "X works", is that "works" backed by the study design, or did I just pick up the authors' optimistic phrasing? That discipline is closer to the root cause than any prompt template.
  • I plan to copy "weaker evidence cannot carry a higher confidence" straight into my content guidelines. It binds evidence strength to conclusion strength — when the evidence is weak, the conclusion has to be weak. A meaningful share of my readers take conclusions straight into action, so if I decouple evidence grade from conclusion strength while paraphrasing, I am manufacturing risk rather than merely being imprecise.
  • Honestly, its own self-limitation is worth watching: the README states outright that it publishes no comparative success rate, no Sentinel interception precision, and no third-party reproduction. That makes it a framework whose design is worth copying, not a performance guarantee to rely on. My attitude should be "copy its structure, do not cite its numbers" — which is itself the rule I set for my own content: only cite first-hand conclusions that trace back to the source.
  • It pairs with the fourth pick in this issue, grainulation/grainulator, which is the same theme from a different angle. Empirica handles the inside question — does the agent really know what it thinks it knows — while grainulator handles the outside — did this task's evidence get recorded and challenged. One governs internal calibration, the other governs external traceability.

2. The evidence-grounded research workbench turns local-first: ScholarSeed bakes traceable citations and agent isolation into the product substrate

URL: https://github.com/alibaba-damo-academy/ScholarSeed ｜ ★50 ｜ Apache-2.0 ｜ an open-source, local-first, AI-native research workbench from Alibaba's DAMO Academy: literature search, PDF reading, source-linked Q&A and review writing, with agents running in an isolated sandbox and research methods encodable as reusable skills

What happened

  • 47 commits today, the highest commit count of the four lines in this issue, with the main thread merging release/0.3.2 back into main. This round concentrates on isolating dependency caches on the desktop side: fix(desktop): isolate Windows vendor component cache inputs, fix(desktop): isolate Windows Python cache inputs, fix(desktop): disable the updater framework signing cache, fix(dev): recover verified dependency leases after shutdown — all pointing the same way: caches from different sources must not contaminate each other.
  • The most valuable passage of the README is Trust & local-first: local SQLite, projects are folders you own, data lives in ~/.scholarseed; agents run in isolated homes with plugins, hooks and marketplaces all disabled, MCP configuration is projected explicitly, secrets are passed only through environment variables, and binaries are pinned by SHA-256.
  • On traceable citations it is unusually restrained: the README states plainly that "citations help you inspect an answer; they do not establish that its claims are correct or fully supported. Research conclusions still require judgment against the original evidence." A citation lets you go back and check; it does not make the conclusion true.
  • Research methods are encoded as Skills. The bundled skill sources include agentic-search, precise-literature-search, literature-review, research-idea-partartner, design-research-validation, pdf-evidence and project-literature; Plugins combine skills with conversation cards (in developer preview); MCP servers connect external tools; the currently included executable agent provider is Codex.
  • It supports Zotero, arXiv, OpenAlex and Elicit as literature sources plus Firecrawl and Brave Search for retrieval, and Alibaba Cloud Model Studio, OpenAI, Anthropic, DeepSeek, Zhipu and Kimi on the model side. The architecture runs Electron/React → FastAPI → Runtime Gateway → Codex Adapter, with DamoATrace handling evidence recording, storage, querying and diagnostics as a separate concern.

Why it matters

  • The combination of "agents run in an isolated home, plugins and hooks disabled, secrets only through environment variables" is exactly what my skill and expert packages inside WorkBuddy should look like. I run skills across many hosts daily; if one skill can quietly attach a hook or read another credential, my entire toolchain has no boundary. Trading isolation for trust is the same move as the "scanner never executes skill code" pattern I noted last issue, expressed as architecture rather than as a feature.
  • The "DamoATrace as a separate evidence layer" architecture is something I should borrow directly into my own content pipeline. My current flow is: AI generates → I check by hand → I edit → it publishes. The audit trail of that checking step is not kept, so next time I face a similar issue I verify it from scratch. If there were an independent evidence-recording layer holding the original text, my judgement and the reason for each edit, content production would become accumulative instead of restarting from zero every time.
  • That sentence about citations is the thing I most want to remember from this issue. "A citation lets you go back and check; it does not make the conclusion true" translates directly into my publishing discipline: appending references does not automatically make something verifiable into something validated. In the GRPM nutrigenomics reports and in "4P Precision Nutrition Watch" I cite literature heavily; if I only attach reference numbers without judging how well the source actually supports the claim, I am conflating checkability with correctness.
  • "Local-first, bring your own model, methods as skills" is precisely the shape I want the "certification hook + nutrition AI kernel lightweight assistant" to take. My GLP-1 nutrition companion already has 153 ingredient templates and 15 functional targets, but that knowledge is currently hard-coded rather than reusable as skills. ScholarSeed shows the other route: write "how to make a nutrition judgement from evidence" as a skill, not as an if-else chain.
  • With 50 stars and 47 commits this is a young project, and I will not treat a nice architecture as a mature solution. But "cache isolation", "sandboxed agents" and "evidence as its own layer" are engineering problems I will hit anyway building the 4P platform — seeing someone else's solution today is cheaper than discovering the same problem myself in six months.

  • The feature that caught my attention most is fake-reference identification: paste a reference list and it checks each entry against authoritative databases to prevent citing AI-fabricated papers. That moves "traceable citations" from "attach a link" to "verify existence one by one".

Why it matters

  • Fake-reference identification is a direct pain point in my own content work, and it solves a problem that damages the brand. When I write nutrition-intervention pieces for the public account, the worst outcome is not "too few references" but citing a paper that looks real and does not exist — AI-generated references are not rare in nutrition popularisation. This tool turns "every reference I cite genuinely exists and can be found in an authoritative database" into a batch check rather than something I eyeball one at a time. It closes the last gap on the citation side of my "zero AI hallucination" rule.
  • Making journal ranking and impact factor default columns is a good product decision. It does not ask the user to go look anything up; it puts the evidence-strength judgement on screen at the moment the library is being organised. My content topic selection should work the same way: the strength of the evidence should be a visible field at the moment I decide whether to cover something, not something I remember to check after writing it.
  • Supporting fully offline Ollama matters at the level of a hard constraint for a one-person company with 8 hours a week and roughly a month of cash runway. I do run qwen2.5:7b locally, but that model cannot search a 240-million-paper source plus a citation network. This puts "works locally" and "evidence-grade retrieval" in the same product, which I had assumed were a trade-off.
  • The four-entry-point design is worth copying. Web app for zero-install, deep Zotero integration, a WeChat mini program for tracking, a browser extension for on-the-spot lookups — that maps exactly onto my own reality of a public account, Xiaohongshu, Obsidian and WorkBuddy. One knowledge base opened from four doors, same source, continuable from anywhere. My GLP-1 nutrition companion currently has a single web entry, which is a real shortcoming.
  • But I must be clear about the limits: the licence is not declared, the core capability depends on its own 240-million-paper library on the service side, and "AI one-click review" is still model generation underneath. The README's "every point tagged with a real reference number" is a product promise, not something I have verified by testing. I would use it for citation checking and nothing else, and would not let it stand in for my own reading of the source.

4. Agent reliability splits into two verifiable layers: grainulator keeps the evidence ledger, Empirica governs internal calibration

URL: https://github.com/grainulation/grainulator ｜ ★87 ｜ MIT ｜ an evidence and verification layer for model-assisted work: a local ledger of typed claims with evidence tiers and provenance, conflict detection, weak-support signals, and separate Auto and Manual next-step lists, used through Claude Code and Codex plugins or over MCP

What happened

  • 10 commits today alongside a v3.1.0-line release, with the work concentrated on MCP quality and rollback safety: Merge pull request #65 from grainulation/codex/mcp-quality, Read native bindings through a pinned file descriptor, Integrate the separate rollback fix beneath MCP quality work, Protect native MCP setup and document profile-safe upgrades.
  • Its capabilities are stated concretely: evidence that travels with the work (typed claims, sources, evidence tiers and provenance that survive edits and exports), a check on the answer (conflict detection, weak-support signals and explicit gaps to investigate), clear next actions (two lists: what the agent can continue automatically, and what needs your input), sessions you can carry forward (configurable research, stop/resume, reusable context and credential-free exports), and verification for your task (a managed command loop that can use a verifier you supply).
  • The work cycle is four steps: record findings with sources and evidence tiers, check for conflicts, weak support and missing perspectives, act on the gaps that matter, then verify by running a managed command loop with your verifier, where a soundness break forces an exit 3.
  • The routing is stated honestly: route 1 (local ledger and offline demo, about 2 seconds, $0, no credentials), route 2 (managed command verifier, with per-round timeout and round cap), route 3 (host plugin or MCP server, $0 in Grainulator overhead) and route 4 (semantic and adversarial evaluations, $0 offline, with the live matrix requiring an explicit budget).
  • The most honest passage in the README is its limits statement: "The compiler checks the structure and support recorded in the ledger; it does not establish that a source is true", and "Evaluations do not establish a general accuracy or efficiency gain, and per-pass limits are not a whole-session spending cap." — it explicitly separates "checking that the ledger's own record is self-consistent" from "whether the source is true".

Why it matters

  • The Auto list / Manual list split is the most practically useful design I saw today, and it maps exactly onto my real situation of eight hours a week. All of my AI work is currently mixed together — nothing separates "what can continue automatically" from "what needs my personal judgement" — so I end up spending real hours on things that should have been automatic, and the decisions that genuinely need me get squeezed out at the end. Making that split explicit has value on its own, even if I never install the tool.
  • Making evidence tier and provenance a recorded field rather than a matter of narrative tone is the same line as the LesionAgent judgement I recorded last issue. Last issue I wrote that "what decides the gap is the framework, not the model". This issue gives that judgement two concrete shapes: Empirica is the inside one (does the agent truly know what it thinks it knows), grainulator is the outside one (did this task's evidence get kept and challenged). What my content production lacks is precisely the latter.
  • The "credential-free export" detail is a hard requirement for anyone who has to move evidence out of the tool. My outputs end up in public-account articles, Word reports and client plans, so the evidence ledger has to be able to exist outside the tool that produced it. That looks like an implementation detail but actually decides whether the method can enter my real workflow.
  • It states plainly that per-pass limits are not a whole-session spending cap, and that kind of self-limitation is rare among comparable projects. That also flags a practical problem I have not solved: my budget constraints for AI must be session-level, not per-call. The model budget I set for WorkBuddy, and my own API spend, have no session-level hard cap — that is the first guardrail I should add, because runaway spend is the one failure mode that is immediately fatal.
  • All ten commits today landed on MCP quality and rollback safety rather than new features, and that is itself a signal. The hard part of tools like this is not building features but making sure that installing a plugin cannot wreck an existing environment — which is exactly what "Protect native MCP setup and document profile-safe upgrades" is about. My own WorkBuddy plugin and expert-package ecosystem will eventually hit the same wall.

URL: https://github.com/averagejoematt/life-platform ｜ ★1 ｜ not declared ｜ an N=1 health intelligence platform built in public: 20+ wearable, app and lab data sources, a deterministic computation pipeline plus an 8-agent AI coaching layer, 96 MCP tools, a hard $215/month budget ceiling, every conclusion labelled with confidence

  • 7 commits today, with the main thread an editorial overhaul plus governance-record alignment: docs(wrap): Editorial Overhaul 11-21→25 — eight second editions, the compact Morning Brief, standing..., fix(digest): the subscribe funnel counts the platform's own tools apart from readers, fix(privacy): two legacy pages are quarantined to the no-longer-available stub and held there by rule, and docs(remediation): the agent is OFF by owner ruling 2026-11-24 — records aligned.

  • The architecture figures in the README are: about 108 Lambdas (ingest → store → serve), 96 MCP tools, a single-table DynamoDB (USER#…#SOURCE#… / DATE#…, with 2 sanctioned GSIs), AWS Bedrock (Claude Sonnet 4.6 for narrative, Haiku 4.5 for structured output, prompt-cached), 10 CDK stacks, and three doors out to the public (Cockpit / Story / Evidence).

  • It draws a very clear line around its own data: the README opens with "everything is correlative (never causal), confidence-labelled, and the down weeks are shown, not hidden." — everything is correlative and never causal, all of it is labelled with confidence, and the weeks that went badly are shown rather than hidden.

Why it matters

  • "The down weeks are shown, not hidden" is the most substantial product decision I read today. This project publishes the weeks that failed. That maps directly onto my own situation with the "Bai-Shi" health project's 2022–2025 participant data: if I write it up, do I write up the successes, or the successes together with the gender-success-rate differences and the ineffective cases?   • "A deterministic computation pipeline plus an 8-agent AI coaching layer" is the same judgement I recorded last issue, expressed as architecture.   • A $215/month hard cap plus a cost governor that degrades AI capability by budget tier is the piece of engineering I most want to copy. The defining constraint on my one-person company has never been "not enough features" but "cost becomes uncontrollable once features are added". My constraint is stated as eight hours of time per week, but my cost constraint exists only as talk, never as mechanism. "When the budget runs short, degrade the AI capability automatically instead of letting the bill explode" should have been in the GLP-1 nutrition companion on day one.
  • The commit that switched the agent off by owner ruling and aligned the records speaks directly to my "4P Precision Nutrition Thousand-Person Pioneer Project", which involves client delivery. For a module that can act on its own: under what conditions must it be switchable off, does switching it off leave a trace, and who has the authority to switch it back on — I have written answers to none of those four questions in the project docs. Yesterday I noted that high-risk operations require second confirmation; today's repo answers it better: governance actions must leave a trail, and the authority to shut down belongs to a named person.
  • Honestly, this project has 1 star, 7 commits today and no declared licence, and the README states plainly that it is "Proprietary — © 2026 Matthew. All rights reserved. Solo-maintained; not open for external contribution." — it is not open source, so I can read it but not copy its code. What I can take away are design judgements (layering, budget degradation, honest disclosure, governance traceability), not implementation. That has to be said clearly, or my readers will assume the code itself is reusable.

3. Trends today

  • ⭐⭐⭐ "Is the agent reliable" was today split into two engineering layers you can land separately. EmpiricaAI/empirica handles the inside: 13 epistemic vectors, memory with evidence tiers and retraction, a Sentinel that sets a criticality-scaled gate between "I think I understand this" and "let me change the code", and confidence aligned against deterministic checks through self-assessed / observed / grounded vectors. grainulation/grainulator handles the outside: a claim ledger with evidence tiers, conflict detection, weak-support prompts, Auto and Manual lists, and exports that need no credentials. Both authors wrote on their own README what they have not proven — Empirica admits to no published comparative success rate or Sentinel interception precision, grainulator admits that the compiler checks the ledger's internal structure and support rather than whether a source is true. That candour is itself a sign of maturity: yesterday I was still noting that "writing evidence strength into the artefact" was a minority practice, and today two unrelated teams are doing it simultaneously, with their own limits written on the front page.
  • ⭐⭐⭐ "Traceable citations" is turning from a promise into several concrete stations on a pipeline. wdcpclover/ai4paper makes "check each reference against authoritative databases and catch AI-fabricated papers" a feature, makes journal partition and impact factor default columns in the item list, and tags every point in a 3,000–5,000 character review with a real reference number; alibaba-damo-academy/ScholarSeed puts citation traceability and evidence as its own layer (DamoATrace) together, and states plainly that "citations help you inspect an answer; they do not establish that its claims are correct"; mims-harvard/ToolUniverse was still today fixing schemas that "claimed an assertion but asserted nothing". All three point the same way: what the AI wrote has to be traceable back to the original text one claim at a time, not just decorated with something that looks like a link. The direct mapping for my work: every public-account article I publish should have its references batch-verified for existence and support before it goes out, rather than praying afterwards that I cited correctly.
  • ⭐⭐⭐ A whole cluster of local-first, traceable-ledger MCP servers appeared on the health and nutrition line today, and their convergence exceeded what I expected. Jihaochang1/foodcalorie-mcp carries Chinese food and chain-restaurant nutrition data (103 free items plus 116 chain menu items across 14 brands, with calorie figures for all 116 menu items and source links); getwimsta/wimsta-mcp gives BMI, body fat %, BMR, TDEE and max heart rate as honest ranges across published formulas rather than one falsely precise number; MetExplore/mcp-metexplore puts INRAE's metabolic-network analysis into the agent loop and has entered the official MCP registry; Jasonatafricanow/Nutrition-Tracker-MCP is a household nutrition ledger with traceable corrections and a local SQLite dashboard; pandefen1983/ingredient-audit-skill is an auditable ingredient-screening agent with multi-platform sources and evidence grading. Five unrelated teams, the same day, each doing the same thing: turn a nutrition fact that normally lives in a PDF or a spreadsheet into an interface an agent can call, with the call recorded. This is close to exactly what my GLP-1 nutrition companion wants to consume — and their shared choice is local SQLite and self-hosted MCP rather than a vendor API.
  • ⭐⭐ "Correlation versus causation" and "what happens when things go badly" are being written into products rather than buried in disclaimers. averagejoematt/life-platform puts on the first screen of its README that everything is correlative and never causal, everything is confidence-labelled, and the down weeks are shown rather than hidden — and treats "the agent is OFF by owner ruling, records aligned" as a formal commit; epic-open-source/seismometer (Epic's open-source medical AI evaluation suite) is not about leaderboard scores but about validating models against your own patient population, assessing fairness across cohorts and analysing the effect of interventions on outcomes; arshleenn-kaurr/clinical-evidence-reasoning builds reproducible benchmarks that test how models reason when clinical evidence conflicts or arrives sequentially. Three repositories from three angles converge on the same point: in health, honestly labelling uncertainty is moving from an ethical posture to an engineering requirement. The direct effect on me: the word "related" has to genuinely appear in my intervention reports, rather than being quietly swapped out for words like "improved" or "increased".
  • ⭐⭐ Agents are starting to be treated as distributed, observable, remotely steerable workforce rather than a chat box. WenyuChiou/agent-board is a local dashboard showing what each agent is doing and which one is waiting on you; gaixianggeng/mimi-remote lets you watch progress, approve actions and change requirements from an iPhone; Eslamasabry/opencode-mobile-next runs Claude Code and OpenCode on Android with the ability to intervene remotely; intentic/intentic describes itself plainly as "more work done, less AI smell"; escoffier-labs/brigadestack is positioned as "run coding agents anywhere and keep one record of the work: shared tasks, claims and progress tracking". This line is especially real for a one-person company with one Mac and eight hours a week: what I lack is not ideas, but the ability to let work already in flight keep running while I am away from the computer, and to see where it is stuck.
