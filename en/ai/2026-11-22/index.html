2. Clinical narrative needs a record behind it: "management started" only when an order exists

URL: https://github.com/TomoOkuyama/clinosim ｜ ★3 ｜ MIT ｜ A clinically realistic hospital data simulator built at InterSystems: forward simulation from a population produces FHIR R4 synthetic EHR, every patient carrying a hidden 19-variable physiological state from which all observations are derived

What happened

  • 48 commits today, shipping v0.7.2 and v0.7.1; almost all of them fall into two families, fix(narrative) and fix(simulator), pointing in a remarkably consistent direction.
  • The most representative is: say "management started" only when an order exists — the phrase is only permitted in the narrative when a corresponding order is present.
  • In the same family: outpatient notes stop asserting unrecorded home logs, and operative notes state real content rather than a fixed skeleton.
  • The physiology side tightens in step: antihypertensives are held during permissive hypertension in acute stroke, mechanical ventilation is modelled after intubation, and newborn bilirubin is labelled as transcutaneous.
  • Japanese prescription sig terms are localised today too: translate and order JP prescription sig terms, plus corrections to emergency exam phrasing and the oxygen flow unit.

Why it matters

  • InterSystems built this and still declares it explicitly not an official product, and the README's first line states that everything is synthetic and no real patient data or PHI is ingested — synthetic data still has to declare its provenance and boundary, which until now I have only seen in personal projects.
  • The most valuable part is the shape of its causal discipline: the data is not written first and the story fitted afterwards. A hidden physiological state comes first, and observations and narrative are derived from it. The most common error in my field is the exact inverse: the conclusion comes first, then supporting language is hunted for. clinosim effectively writes "a conclusion must hang on a fact that actually exists" into the generator itself.
  • The rule that management started only appears when an order exists can be moved into my 4P intervention report without changing a word: "already advised" and "already carried out" are two different statements and must not be blurred. The boundary between them is not yet hard enough in my current report phrasing.
  • One more transferable pattern: its narrative and simulator fixes are kept as separate families — wording in the narrative layer and state in the data layer have separate owners. If I split the quality attribution of my reports into three parallel tracks — judgement layer, data layer, presentation layer — this is a ready-made sample.

URL: https://github.com/timor-m/fnos-app-health-records ｜ ★55 ｜ MIT ｜ A family-facing health report archive, deployable on fnOS or Docker, with OCR and AI switchable on demand

What happened

  • 7 commits today, shipping v0.3.2 and v0.3.1.
  • On the indicator side, today's work adds two-way unit conversion for thyroid function: free triiodothyronine (FT3) and total thyroxine (T4), with matching test cases updated.
  • The health-archive indicator dictionary was also updated, adding definitions for myoglobin and neutrophil-related entries, again with tests.
  • On delivery shape, v0.3.2 reworked the storage settings page layout and responsiveness; v0.3.1 removed the port configuration item from the install and setup wizard and updated the related documentation and scripts.
  • The README states the boundary plainly: AI assists in organising material but does not replace a doctor's diagnosis or treatment advice; OCR and AI are both optional, and records and originals stay in your own NAS or Docker data directory; names, ID numbers and contact details are de-identified before report text is sent to a cloud model; family members are filed separately and even the system administrator does not automatically get access to everyone's private archive; key create/edit/delete actions leave an audit trail, and you can check line by line which model the AI used, how many tokens it consumed, how long it took and whether it finished.

Why it matters

  • This is a ready-made open-source reference for a path I am already building: version 61 of my plan is photo → OCR → AI → App import of lab reports, and this project has that chain working — snap a photo, import HEIC or multi-page PDFs, bulk-import from a NAS, let OCR/AI on demand organise dates, conclusions, indicators and medications, and keep the original alongside.
  • Its "original provenance" design is the most valuable part for me: from an indicator back to the source page, locating the original evidence, with missing entries fillable, results editable and sources selectable. Right now my GLP-1 nutrition companion has structured values after OCR but no way to pin each number back to the exact block of the report that produced it — when a client questions a reading I cannot show them the row in the original image.
  • Its "manual-correction protection" is finer than most projects: re-processing preserves manual corrections by matching them to their source, and key edits surface a conflict when a concurrent change happened, so an old form cannot silently overwrite newer content. This is "a machine re-run must not eat the human's confirmation" — something my own path into persisting every interaction for self-evolution learning will eventually run into.
  • Updating the indicator dictionary with myoglobin and neutrophil definitions plus two-way FT3/T4 conversion with tests — unit conversion must be bidirectional and tested, and that closes a real gap in how I handle thyroid indicators.

4. "Not proven means not dead": a code retrieval tool writes evidence strength into its answers

URL: https://github.com/jgravelle/jcodemunch-mcp ｜ ★2,858 ｜ not declared ｜ A symbol-level code retrieval MCP: uses tree-sitter AST parsing to fetch code by symbol rather than by file, returning the function you need instead of the whole file

What happened

  • 52 commits today, shipping six consecutive versions from v1.108.477 through v1.108.482. The release notes are themselves today's theme: a Go file no go.mod covers is never proven dead, and a C or C++ caller list says it came from a text match.
  • The implementation follows with two matching rules: find_dead_code reads the go.mod module map, not directory names, and one go.mod rule serves both find_dead_code and check_delete_safe.
  • On the C/C++ side, four more caller layouts are covered and the answer states plainly that the match is text-only; the C name table is also compressed into a single value, published once.
  • The README gives a reproducible benchmark: five queries each on express, fastapi and gin at pinned commits, tokens counted with tiktoken cl100k_base, averaging 96.3% fewer tokens per lookup (91.1%–99.2% across individual lookups), with benchmarks/REPRODUCING.md provided. The README's production-side figures (838B+ tokens, 136,000+ installs, $4.2M+ spend avoided) are labelled as estimates dated 2026-08-17 rather than live statistics.

Why it matters

  • "A Go file that no go.mod covers can never be judged dead" is exactly the rule my field lacks: a verdict has to hang on verifiable evidence rather than on a plausible-sounding heuristic. It is the other end of the same line as "the benchmark itself must be open to challenge" that I picked up from nautilus-compass last issue.
  • "When a caller list only matches on text, the answer says so" is evidence grading: not found / not found, but real call / text-only hit / inferred from the module map. All 24 risk dimensions in my 4P intervention report are currently binary. This can be turned directly into three columns — conclusion, evidence strength, evidence source.
  • 96.3% fewer tokens matters directly under my one-person-company constraints of eight hours a week and roughly a month of cash runway: reading whole files instead of reading symbols is a direct cost lever, since the same budget buys more rounds of verification. The README also documents how to reproduce the number (pinned commits, public benchmark scripts), which is why I would adopt it — a figure you can reproduce is a figure worth copying into a spec.
  • GitHub could not identify this repository's licence (recorded as "not declared"), but the description states non-commercial use is free while commercial use requires a one-time licence — this is the one item whose authorisation boundary I still need to confirm myself. The other 159 entries carry an explicit SPDX licence: MIT, Apache-2.0, AGPL-3.0, BSD-3-Clause, CC0-1.0, CC-BY-4.0, MPL-2.0 and so on.

5. Acceptance separate from execution: verifylens enters JetBrains and separates "state was set" from "state was checked"

URL: https://github.com/DamunKorea/verifylens ｜ ★0 ｜ MIT ｜ Judge per requirement whether an AI-generated change is verified, different, or not yet verified — runs locally with no LLM

What happened

  • 28 commits today, shipping v0.21.0 and the first JetBrains preview 0.15.0 (carrying the same 0.21.0 engine); the previous two days already shipped jetbrains 0.14.0 and 0.14.1, fixing projects opened through a mapped drive or a link.
  • The core rule of 0.21.0: a block that only sets a state is not the check for it — code that merely assigns a value is not a verification of that value. This is the same problem as the "tests changed to pass" detection already shipped in the 2026-11-21 version, seen from the other side.
  • On the JetBrains side, a README status table was added; the CLI's --lang now follows the system language when unset; the CLI package check now installs this commit from its tarball; and npx -y github:DamunKorea/verifylens runs without installing.
  • The state machine is explicitly three-state rather than binary: verified / different / not yet verified.

Why it matters

  • This is the single design I most want to copy today: verification must be judged by a stage independent of the implementation, and the verdict has to distinguish verified, different and not yet verified — "not yet verified" must not be folded into either "problem found" or "no problem". I picked up the idea of a third state last issue, but today's project supplies the concrete criterion: what counts as verification? Setting a state is not verifying a state.
  • My 4P expert package runs 30–50 two-way comparison evaluations with both the questions and the scoring in my own hands — precisely where "changed the test so it goes green" is most likely. verifylens's three-state verdict plus the fact that it runs locally with no model means this line of defence can sit permanently in CI at zero token cost.
  • "Separating execution from acceptance" is not one project's move today: Innei/ryugi states that only results of the work become claims and routine checks are one line; CopperTide/Shakedown makes a rewindable copy of the world so runs can be forked from any step and compared; srpadmanabhuni/agentest runs 165 offline deterministic tests with no LLM judge; MontrealAI/proof-gradient has every run leave proof; YusefSyed/tool-effect-bench scores prompt injection from sandbox database state rather than from the transcript. Five unrelated projects converging on "execution and acceptance must be separate" in one week is a density worth recording.
  • MIT licence, zero stars, small codebase — which is exactly the kind of small, hard verification tool a one-person company should copy first: not by adopting a platform, but by copying one decision rule into my own spec.

3. Trends today

  • ⭐⭐⭐ Evidence strength written into the artifact itself has become today's most complete through-line. At least six unrelated projects did the same thing today — make "how solid is my conclusion" explicit rather than leaving readers to guess. TomoOkuyama/clinosim requires an order to exist before the narrative may say management started, and stops outpatient notes asserting unrecorded home logs; jgravelle/jcodemunch-mcp rules that a Go file no go.mod covers is never dead code and says so when a C/C++ caller list is text-only; Steinbeck-Lab/Orthonym checks names by an OPSIN round trip and declines rather than guess; Kinprove/genealogy-research-recipes splits reviewed source records from still-pending ones in its provenance report; mims-harvard/ToolUniverse fixed eleven schemas that claimed an assertion and asserted nothing; ryuxdsgn/design-intelligence checks that a report's gate line counts the same areas as its detail. Six teams, six ecosystems, one idea. The most direct mapping for my field: every conclusion in my intervention report should be able to answer three questions — what is it based on, how strong is that evidence, and what has not yet been verified. I have none of the three today.
  • ⭐⭐⭐ Refusing beats guessing, and the refusal must fail closed. A group of projects today turned "not doing it" into explicit behaviour rather than silently allowing it: the-omics-os/lobster's write_file refuses to overwrite a file the session already loaded, exports refuse likewise, and imported files are identified by identity rather than by path string; DylanCosto/pasta fails on corrupt codec streams instead of panicking; Exelka/ExelCode stops the agent loop on repeated failures with no file change; clinosim's narrative states only what the record contains. This is a fuller version of the "store 0 as null" rule I recorded last issue — not merely "record a missing value as missing", but "when evidence is insufficient, the artifact must refuse to be produced rather than producing something plausible". My GLP-1 nutrition companion still emits a number when a reading cannot be trusted.
  • ⭐⭐ Deterministic baselines are becoming the default rather than an option. srpadmanabhuni/agentest's 165 tests are offline, deterministic and need no LLM judge; YusefSyed/tool-effect-bench scores prompt injection on tool-using agents from sandbox database state rather than from the transcript (188 samples, 17 models, 5,628 scored runs); anandapurva55/multi-source-mcp-benchmark puts MCP servers in a no-network Docker sandbox for conflicting-evidence evaluation; CopperTide/Shakedown supports forking from any step of a rewindable world copy; MontrealAI/proof-gradient has every run leave proof. These are two sides of the same question as "who is allowed to score whom". My 4P expert package's two-way comparison evaluation is currently a fully self-referential loop — LLM writes the questions, LLM writes the answers, LLM does the scoring — and these five projects today all point at the same conclusion: a self-scoring loop needs external pins (deterministic tests, state snapshots, independent assertions), otherwise editing the questions is enough to edit out a good score.
  • ⭐⭐ Determinism of units and thresholds is the main activity on the health line today. timor-m/fnos-app-health-records added two-way unit conversion for FT3 and T4 and updated the indicator dictionary with myoglobin and neutrophil definitions; noharm-ai/backend stopped the max-dose total from double-counting the schema's list of frequencies; OpenStrap/edge lowered the floor of its 0–100 stress score so bad nights stop reading as 0; youssofal/MTPLX restructured its README to lead with measured speeds and which pack fits which Mac. All four are the same kind of fix: shutting down the possibility that the same quantity means different things in different places. Unit conversion and threshold judgement are where my supplement plans are easiest to get wrong and hardest to notice — bidirectional conversion plus tests, both conditions required.
  • ⭐ Deliverables now explicitly label "this part is approximate". alchaincyf/huashu-design states that its own comparison images are approximate and that the real effect is for the user to click through in the preview card, and that the delivery package states which characters use non-system fonts so font fallback does not break lines; Innei/ryugi states that only results of the work become claims and routine checks are one line; brmel/farkad-ai's report prints p50 and p95 seconds along with the cached share of input; industrial-aiops/industrial-aiops separates the "0.35 reference scenario" from "candidate trials" in its documentation. The words "approximate" and "reference" appear repeatedly in deliverables today — a supplement I have not seen elsewhere: not only "if it wasn't run, don't report a number", but also "if it was run but is only a reference, label it a reference".
