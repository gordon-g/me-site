ENA Tufts University study in JAND analyzed NHANES data from 30,000 US adults: between 2003 and 2018 coffee intake stayed flat at about 13 ounces daily, yet calories from coffee rose nearly 40% from 43 to 60 kcal/day. Non-dairy creamer consumption more than doubled, overtaking sugar and milk as the leading source of added calories — a hidden-calorie entry point worth attention in health management.
